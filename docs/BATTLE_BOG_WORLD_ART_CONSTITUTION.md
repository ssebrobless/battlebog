# Battle Bog World Art Constitution

Status: research-derived prototype targets

Compiled: 2026-07-27

## World Formula

```text
SUPERVIVE terrain hierarchy
          +
Albion wetland materials
          +
Wild Woods route framing
          +
restrained Ravenswatch night
          |
          v
lush reactive wetland + competitive readability
```

Evercore remains a supporting source for bright dimensional finish and colored
night fill. No single reference supplies the shipped visual identity.

## Spatial Hierarchy

Every visible world element belongs to one functional layer:

1. playable ground;
2. terrain-affecting ground;
3. collision blocker;
4. vision or cover element;
5. harvestable or objective;
6. animated ambient layer;
7. suppressible combat decoration.

Art must reveal this ownership through silhouette, value, edge treatment and
motion before labels are added.

## Density Targets

| Region | Starting Target |
| --- | --- |
| Objective combat floor | at least `60%` low-frequency unobstructed terrain |
| Central combat props | at most `15-20%` occupied by decorative/blocking detail |
| Route and habitat edges | approximately `30-40%` prop occupancy |
| Ecological clusters | irregular groups of `3-7` related props with negative space |
| Reactive nearby props | `15-30%`; keep `70-85%` static or cached |

Lushness comes from clustering, material variation, depth and selective motion,
not uniform prop coverage or brighter saturation.

## Shoreline Grammar

Every important shore should expose:

`dry ground -> wet mud -> shallow water -> deep water`

Starting scale:

- wet fringe: `0.25-0.5` creature diameters;
- shallow band: `0.5-1` creature diameter;
- shallow body masking: about `15-30%`;
- deep-swimming presentation: about `35-60%`, while targeting and truth rings
  remain readable.

Water entry:

- first response within `100 ms`;
- disturbance lasts approximately `0.6-1.2 s`;
- wake extends `1-3` body lengths according to speed and mass.

The map needs irregular natural banks while preserving readable crossing,
cover and depth boundaries.

## Reactive Ecology

Environment responses are event-driven where practical:

- nearby grass, reeds and lilies bend or part;
- mud and shallow water retain short-lived travel evidence;
- small insects or wildlife react locally;
- heavy movement produces larger but less frequent response;
- combat suppression reduces nonessential reactions first.

Vegetation contact should begin within `100 ms` and settle over roughly
`0.5-1.5 s`.

Static terrain remains cached. Continuous ambient movement is throttled and
spatially bounded.

## Habitat Identity

Every habitat or encounter zone receives:

- one landmark silhouette;
- one substrate signature;
- one characteristic water shape;
- one ambient-motion signature.

These identifiers must remain useful in grayscale and without relying on a
single green/brown hue distinction.

## Night

Night changes information and mood without making play physically unreadable.

Starting target:

- reduce median terrain value by only `20-30%`;
- retain creature, team and telegraph contrast;
- use colored local fill and altered ambient activity;
- strengthen uncertainty through visibility state and sound/ripple events;
- avoid black vignettes and broad opaque darkness.

Ravenswatch is a nighttime boundary study, not the baseline. Heroes of the
Storm remains useful for world-state transformation. SUPERVIVE contributes
uncertain information rather than darkness.

## Objective Aftermath

Major objectives should leave at least two visible consequences for `8-20 s`:

- one ground or water residue;
- one motion, occupancy or ecological change.

Examples include disturbed reeds, churned mud, lingering wake, temporarily
vacated wildlife, broken cover or a changed local ambient pattern. These
effects communicate history without becoming permanent clutter.

## Construction Strategy

Prefer:

- chunked terrain;
- shoreline and substrate decals;
- reusable prop atlases;
- cached static layers;
- event-driven reactive layers;
- combat-density suppression;
- Battle Bog truth rings and telegraphs above environment art.

Avoid one full-map painting whose details cannot respond, suppress or align
with gameplay ownership.

## Failure Conditions

World art fails when:

- traversable and non-traversable edges merge;
- decoration competes with team or danger saturation;
- objective floor detail hides bodies or projectiles;
- lushness is achieved through uniform visual frequency;
- water depth depends on hue alone;
- night requires brightness settings to remain playable;
- ambient motion continues at full intensity during dense combat;
- shoreline art does not align with medium transition gameplay;
- a habitat is identifiable only by labels or minimap icons.
